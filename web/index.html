Overview

What the data says

One protocol, run on two real trials. Every number below comes from the analysis scripts in this repository; the prediction model was trained on the training split only.

Kaplan-Meier survival

Probability of staying event-free over time

Key findings

  • Supported
    The average benefit is real. All three ACTG 175 combination arms beat ZDV alone (adjusted HR 0.45 to 0.56). Hormonal therapy lowers the hazard in GBSG2 (adjusted HR 0.68).
  • Supported
    A simple model is enough. A ridge Cox model matched or beat random forests and boosting in both trials.
  • Not supported
    Patient-specific benefit. After honest validation, no patient-level difference could be confirmed in either trial.
  • Caution
    Proportional hazards failed for two ACTG 175 arms, so days gained (RMST) and absolute risk are reported next to hazard ratios.

How to read these numbers
Hazard ratio (HR)

Below 1 favours the new arm. An HR of 0.45 means the event rate is about 55% lower at any moment.

RMST gain

Average extra days a patient spends event-free during the first 900 days. It remains meaningful when hazards are not proportional.

NNT

Number needed to treat: patients who must receive the arm for one extra patient to be event-free at 900 days.

Adjusted

Cox estimate after accounting for baseline covariates such as CD4, CD8, age and symptoms.

Effect inside subgroups

Hazard ratio of any new arm versus ZDV. Left of the line favours treatment.

Arms against each other

Pairwise log-rank tests, adjusted for multiple comparisons.

All three combination arms differ from ZDV alone, but they are not clearly different from each other.

Metric glossary
C-index

For two random patients, how often the model correctly says who has the event first. Values of 0.65 to 0.75 are typical in trial data.

AUC at horizon

How well the model separates patients who had an event by the horizon from those who did not.

IBS skill

Probability accuracy compared with a model that predicts one average curve for everyone. 0 means no gain.

Tuning grid (ACTG 175, cross-validation)

Questions 3 and 4

Does benefit differ between patients, and is it real?

Five learners estimate a patient-level effect (CATE). Each is tested against a baseline that gives everybody the same average effect.

Estimated benefit per patient

Days gained versus ZDV (ACTG 175). Bars show the 10th to 90th percentile.

Methods differ widely in spread. Large spread is not evidence of real heterogeneity: flexible models also find "differences" in pure noise.

Validation gain over constant effect

Positive means the patient-level model predicts better than one average effect. An interval that crosses 0 is no evidence.

Verdict: not confirmed

Every gain interval on the test set crosses zero. Some calibration slopes look positive on the test set, but the doubly-robust learners show the opposite sign (negative slopes) on out-of-fold training predictions, so the pattern does not replicate. With 104 test events in ACTG 175 and 60 in GBSG2, no evidence of heterogeneity is not evidence of no heterogeneity. Larger trials are needed.

Prediction

Read before using. This is a ridge Cox model trained on public teaching copies of two trials (1,711 and 548 training patients). Probabilities describe these trial populations, not an individual. The difference between treatments for one patient is the model's estimate only: the validation stage found no reliable evidence of patient-level treatment differences. It is a research demonstration, not clinical decision support.
